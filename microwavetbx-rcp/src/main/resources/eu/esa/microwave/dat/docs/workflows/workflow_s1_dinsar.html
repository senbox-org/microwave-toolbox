SAR Workflows: Sentinel-1 IW DInSAR

Sentinel-1 IW DInSAR (Classical TOPS Chain)

From two Sentinel-1 IW SLC products of the same track you get a differential interferogram, coherence, unwrapped phase and line-of-sight displacement on a map grid. This is the classical chain: coregister the bursts in radar geometry with orbits, a DEM and Enhanced Spectral Diversity, form the interferogram, then deburst and geocode last.

Before you start

  • Data: two IW SLC products from the same relative orbit that cover the same bursts. Use the shortest temporal baseline you can, and check the perpendicular baseline with Radar > Interferometric > InSAR Stack Overview.
  • Orbits and DEM: precise orbits (about 20 days after acquisition) and the Copernicus 30 m DEM are downloaded automatically.
  • Unwrapping: SNAPHU runs outside the SNAP operators; install it or let a SNAPHU tool download it.
  • Ready-made graphs: Radar > Coregistration > S1 TOPS Coregistration > S-1 TOPS Coregistration with ESD runs steps 1 to 4 on a pair. In the Graph Builder, Graphs > Radar > InSAR Graphs has TOPSAR Coreg Interferogram (one sub-swath) and TOPSAR Coreg Interferogram IW All Swaths (three sub-swaths, merged). Neither contains ESD; add it. TOPSAR Slices Coreg Interferogram assembles slices first and includes ESD.

Steps

  1. S-1 TOPS Split (TOPSAR-Split, Radar > Sentinel-1 TOPS; help), on each product. Choose the Subswath (IW1 to IW3) and the bursts: First/Last Burst Index default to 1 and 9999 (all), or give a WKT Area of Interest. Keep at least two bursts so that ESD has an overlap.
  2. Apply Orbit File (Apply-Orbit-File; help), on each product; default Sentinel Precise (Auto Download). The menu graph S-1 TOPS Split Apply Orbit does steps 1 and 2 together. Optionally run S-1 ETAD Correction here, on both dates or on neither.
  3. S-1 Back Geocoding (Back-Geocoding, Radar > Coregistration > S1 TOPS Coregistration; help). The first product is the Reference. Both must be split products of the same sub-swath and polarisation. Defaults: DEM Copernicus 30m Global DEM, Resampling Type BISINC_5_POINT_INTERPOLATION.
  4. S-1 Enhanced Spectral Diversity (Enhanced-Spectral-Diversity, Radar > Coregistration > S1 TOPS Coregistration; help). The defaults suit a pair: Coherence Threshold for Outlier Removal 0.3, 10 windows per overlap, ESD Estimator Periodogram. In low-coherence scenes few windows pass the threshold; check the per-overlap estimates before you lower it.
  5. Interferogram Formation (Interferogram, Radar > Interferometric > Products; help). Subtract flat-earth phase is on (polynomial degree 5, 501 points) and Output coherence estimation is on, with a window of 10 range pixels. In the dialog, Square Pixel (on) fills in the azimuth size (about 3 for IW); from gpt the default window is 10 × 10. Coherence Window (m) overrides both with a window that is square on the ground. Subtract topographic phase is off by default; either tick it here or run step 7.
  6. S-1 TOPS Deburst (TOPSAR-Deburst; help). If you processed several sub-swaths separately, join them now with S-1 TOPS Merge.
  7. Topographic Phase Removal (TopoPhaseRemoval, Radar > Interferometric > Products; help). Default DEM Copernicus 30m Global DEM; use the DEM of step 3. The elevation and lat/lon output bands (off by default) are only needed for StaMPS export.
  8. Multilooking (Multilook, Radar > SAR Utilities; help). With GR Square Pixel on (default) you set the range looks and the azimuth looks follow. For IW, 4 range looks give 1 azimuth look (pixels of about 15 m), 8 give 2 (about 30 m). Output Intensity is off by default, so i and q are averaged: a complex multilook.
  9. Goldstein Phase Filtering (GoldsteinPhaseFiltering, Radar > Interferometric > Filtering; help). Use adaptive alpha is on by default; it uses the coherence band if present, otherwise the fixed exponent alpha (1.0). FFT Size 64 and Window Size 3 are the defaults; FFT Block Extent (m) makes the block square on the ground.
  10. Unwrapping: Snaphu Export (SnaphuExport, Radar > Interferometric > Unwrapping), then SNAPHU with the command in the header of snaphu.conf (or Snaphu Unwrapping), then Snaphu Import. Keep Statistical-cost mode DEFO (default). The default 10 × 10 tiles with 200 pixels of overlap are too many for a small multilooked image; use fewer.
  11. Phase to Displacement (PhaseToDisplacement; help). No parameters. It writes displacement in metres from the first band whose name starts with "unw" and subtracts no reference point.
  12. Range-Doppler Terrain Correction (Terrain-Correction; help). Geocode the displacement product. To geocode the interferogram and coherence too, run Terrain Correction on the Goldstein output: with no bands selected, an InSAR product is geocoded as complex i/q, so the wrapped phase stays correct.

Check the result

  • Burst seams: in Phase_ifg after debursting, look across the seams between bursts. A phase jump along every seam means azimuth misregistration: ESD was skipped or failed.
  • ESD: the shifts are stored in the ESD Measurement metadata element. The per-overlap values should agree. The S-1 Double Difference Interferogram of the overlaps should be noise around zero.
  • Coherence: high on towns and bare rock, low on vegetation, near the estimator floor on water.
  • Residual fringes: after topographic phase removal, fringes that follow the terrain point to a DEM or geometry problem. A smooth ramp across the scene is usually orbit error or ionosphere.
  • Unwrapping: steps of exactly 2π along low-coherence lines are unwrapping errors.

Common pitfalls

  • Debursting before Back-Geocoding (it is refused) or before ESD (it needs the burst overlaps).
  • Selecting different bursts for the two dates, or a single burst. ESD then has no common overlap.
  • Running Thermal Noise Removal on the SLCs. It outputs intensity and destroys the phase.
  • Reading the displacement as absolute or vertical. It is line-of-sight motion relative to an arbitrary constant; subtract a stable reference area.
  • Selecting the virtual Phase band explicitly in Terrain Correction. The wrapped phase is then interpolated across ±π.
