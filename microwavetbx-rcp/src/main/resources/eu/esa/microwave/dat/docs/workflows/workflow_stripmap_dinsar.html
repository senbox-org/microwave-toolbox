SAR Workflows: Stripmap DInSAR

Stripmap DInSAR

This chain makes a differential interferogram, coherence and line-of-sight displacement from two stripmap SLC products, for example TerraSAR-X/TanDEM-X/PAZ, ALOS-2, COSMO-SkyMed, Sentinel-1 SM (Coregistration graph only) or ERS/ENVISAT. Stripmap images have no bursts, so coregistration by cross-correlation and a warp polynomial (or by orbits and a DEM) is accurate enough, and no ESD step is needed.

Before you start

  • Data: two SLC products of the same mission, beam and track. Check the perpendicular and temporal baselines with Radar > Interferometric > InSAR Stack Overview.
  • Orbits: Apply Orbit File handles Sentinel-1, ENVISAT, ERS and KOMPSAT-5 orbit files. For other missions (TerraSAR-X, ALOS-2, COSMO-SkyMed and others) it stops with "... is not suitable for a ... product"; the orbit annotated in the product is used, so skip that step.
  • DEM: the Copernicus 30 m DEM is downloaded automatically by DEM-based steps.
  • Ready-made graphs (Graph Builder, Graphs > Radar > InSAR Graphs): Deformation-Pre-Processing and Deformation-Pre-Processing-Snaphu run Apply Orbit File, Create Stack, Cross-Correlation, Warp, Interferogram, Topographic Phase Removal and Goldstein filtering, and end with Write or SNAPHU Export. They contain no multilooking step. Stripmap-Coregistration-With-Demodulation wraps Cross-Correlation and Warp in Demodulate/Remodulate.

Steps

  1. Apply Orbit File (Apply-Orbit-File; help), on each product, for the missions listed above. For Sentinel-1 the default is Sentinel Precise (Auto Download). For ENVISAT and ERS you can pick DORIS, DELFT or PRARE; a Sentinel orbit type left in a graph is switched automatically to DORIS VOR (ENVISAT) or PRARE (ERS).
  2. Coregistration (Radar > Coregistration > Coregistration; help). This menu graph runs Create Stack (CreateStack, Resampling Type NONE), Cross-Correlation (Cross-Correlation) and Warp (Warp). The graph switches on fine registration and Optimize for InSAR, which are off in the bare operator. Warp defaults: Warp Polynomial Order 2, significance level 0.05 for outlier removal, interpolation Cubic convolution (6 points).
    In steep terrain use DEM Assisted Coregistration (DEM-Assisted-Coregistration, Radar > Coregistration > DEM-Assisted Coregistration) instead. It computes the offsets from orbits and the DEM (default Copernicus 30m Global DEM, resampling BISINC_5_POINT_INTERPOLATION). The with XCorr variant refines them by cross-correlation. It refuses every Sentinel-1 SLC, stripmap (SM) included. For Sentinel-1 IW use the TOPS chain; for Sentinel-1 SM use the Coregistration graph above (Back Geocoding accepts only TOPS burst products).
  3. Spectral filtering, optional (Radar > Interferometric > Filtering > Spectral Filtering: Range Filtering, Azimuth Filtering). Range common-band filtering recovers coherence for large perpendicular baselines; azimuth filtering helps when the two Doppler centroids differ.
  4. Interferogram Formation (Interferogram; help). Keep Subtract flat-earth phase on (default). The coherence window defaults to 10 range pixels; set Coherence Window (m) for a window that is square on the ground. Subtract topographic phase is off by default.
  5. Topographic Phase Removal (TopoPhaseRemoval; help), default DEM Copernicus 30m Global DEM, or tick Subtract topographic phase in step 4.
  6. Multilooking (Multilook; help). Both look numbers default to 1. With GR Square Pixel on (default), choose the range looks and the azimuth looks follow. Aim for ground pixels a few times larger than the fringe noise you want to suppress, but small enough that each fringe still spans several pixels.
  7. Goldstein Phase Filtering (GoldsteinPhaseFiltering; help), with adaptive alpha (default on).
  8. Unwrapping with Snaphu Export (cost mode DEFO, default), SNAPHU, and Snaphu Import.
  9. Phase to Displacement (PhaseToDisplacement; help), then Range-Doppler Terrain Correction (Terrain-Correction).

Check the result

  • Coregistration: tick Show Residuals in Warp. Check how many GCPs survived and the residual RMS. Few GCPs or a large RMS mean low coherence or a poor initial offset.
  • Coherence: compare it with what the land cover and temporal baseline lead you to expect. Low coherence everywhere, even on towns, points to a coregistration failure.
  • Fringes: after topographic phase removal, fringes that follow the terrain mean a DEM or geometry problem. A linear ramp across the scene is usually orbit error. At L-band, broad ramps and streaks are often ionospheric.

Common pitfalls

  • Leaving the Apply-Orbit-File nodes of the ready-made graphs in place for TerraSAR-X, ALOS-2 or COSMO-SkyMed. The graph then stops; delete those nodes.
  • Unwrapping a full-resolution interferogram straight from the Deformation-Pre-Processing-Snaphu graph. Insert Multilook before Goldstein filtering, or the noise produces many residues.
  • Using a large perpendicular baseline for deformation. DEM errors then appear as phase.
  • Applying azimuth filtering or this chain to Sentinel-1 IW data. Use the TOPS chain.
  • Ignoring the ionosphere at L-band. See Ionospheric Correction (Radar > Interferometric > Products).
  • Reading displacement without a reference area. Subtract a stable area first.
