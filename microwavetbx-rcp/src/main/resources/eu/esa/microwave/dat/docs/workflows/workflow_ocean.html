SAR Workflows: Ocean Applications

Ocean Applications: Wind, Ships and Oil

Three short chains turn a calibrated SAR image of the sea into a wind field, a list of ship detections, or a mask of possible oil slicks. All three start the same way: mask the land and calibrate to σ0. Input: a detected product such as Sentinel-1 IW/EW GRD; wind estimation also accepts ERS, ENVISAT and RADARSAT-2.

Before you start

  • Data: a GRD product over open water, in radar geometry. Wind estimation needs a VV or HH band and the incidence_angle tie-point grid, so run it before any terrain correction.
  • Ready-made graphs: the three menu entries under Radar > SAR Applications > Ocean are graphs. The same chains are in the Graph Builder under Graphs > Radar > SAR Applications (WindFieldEstimation, ShipDetection, OilSpillDetection). The menu graphs use some settings that differ from the operator defaults; they are noted below.

Common first steps

  1. Land/Sea Mask (Land-Sea-Mask, Raster > Masks). Mask the Land is on by default. Use SRTM 3sec is on by default: pixels where SRTM has no data are treated as sea. SRTM covers only about 60°N to 56°S, so outside that band nothing is masked. There, untick it (the coarse ACE2 5-minute model is used) or supply a vector. Extend shoreline by this many pixels defaults to 0; the ship and oil menu graphs use 10.
  2. Calibrate (Calibration, Radar > Radiometric; help) to Sigma0 (the default output). All three applications refuse uncalibrated input.

Wind field

  1. Wind Field Estimation (Wind-Field-Estimation, Radar > SAR Applications > Ocean; help). Window Size (km) defaults to 20. In each window the direction comes from the wind streaks and the speed from the CMOD5 model. The result is a WindField vector (speed, heading) and a <product>_wind_field_report.xml in the SNAP log folder. Smaller windows give more detail but fewer streaks to fix the direction.

Ship detection

  1. Adaptive Thresholding (AdaptiveThresholding), a two-parameter CFAR detector. Its windows are in metres: target 50, guard 500, background 800 (defaults). The guard window must be larger than the largest ship. PFA is entered as the exponent x of 10−x: 6.5 by default, 12.5 in the menu graph. With no band selected it picks HV, then VH, then HH if available.
  2. Object Discrimination (Object-Discrimination). It keeps targets between Minimum Target Size and Maximum Target Size: 50 and 600 m by default; the menu graph uses 30 m as the minimum. It writes the ShipDetections vector and an object-detection report.

Run both through Radar > SAR Applications > Ocean > Ocean Object Detection (help).

Oil spill detection

  1. Oil Spill Detection (Oil-Spill-Detection; help). A pixel is flagged when it is darker than the local background mean by more than the Threshold Shift (2.0 dB, default). Background Window Dimension (km) is 0.5 by default and 4.5 in the menu graph; it must be larger than the slicks you look for. Output: <band>_oil_spill_bit_msk.
  2. Oil Spill Clustering (Oil-Spill-Clustering). It removes clusters smaller than Minimum Cluster Size (sq km), 0.1 by default.

Check the result

  • Wind: compare speed and direction with a weather-model or scatterometer wind. The streak direction is ambiguous by 180°.
  • Ships: compare detections with AIS. Detections in a line along the coast mean the land mask is too tight; clusters in rough water mean the PFA exponent is too small.
  • Oil: check the local wind. Slicks are usually only visible in a moderate wind; about 3 to 10 m/s is commonly quoted. Low-wind areas, rain cells, biogenic films and upwelling look the same.

Common pitfalls

  • Skipping the land mask. Coasts, piers and islands become ships and the wind windows mix in land.
  • Relying on the SRTM mask in polar seas. Above 60°N nothing is masked.
  • Treating every dark patch as oil. The detector finds dark spots; deciding what they are needs wind, shape and context.
  • Taking the PFA as the real false-alarm rate. Sea clutter has heavier tails than the Gaussian model.
  • Entering the oil background window in pixels. It is in kilometres.
