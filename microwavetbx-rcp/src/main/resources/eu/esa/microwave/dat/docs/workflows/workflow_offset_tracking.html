SAR Workflows: Offset Tracking

Offset Tracking for Glaciers and Landslides

Offset tracking measures how far image features moved between two dates by cross-correlating small image patches. It uses amplitude, not phase, so it works where InSAR decorrelates and for motion of metres to tens of metres: glaciers, ice streams, fast landslides. The result is a velocity map in metres per day. Input: two detected (GRD) or SLC products of the same track and geometry.

Before you start

  • Data: two images from the same relative orbit. For Sentinel-1 use GRD: DEM Assisted Coregistration refuses Sentinel-1 SLC, and on slant-range products the range offsets are converted with the slant-range spacing.
  • Time interval: the motion must be clearly larger than the matching noise (a fraction of a pixel), but the surface must still look similar on both dates. Fresh snow, melt or crevasse changes break the match; short intervals help.
  • Orbits and DEM: precise orbits and the Copernicus 30 m DEM (default) are downloaded automatically.
  • Graphs: the menu entry Radar > Coregistration > DEM-Assisted Coregistration > DEM Assisted Coregistration is itself a small graph (product set, DEM-Assisted-Coregistration, Write). SNAP ships no graph for the whole chain.

Steps

  1. Apply Orbit File (Apply-Orbit-File; help) on both products; default Sentinel Precise (Auto Download). A spatial subset around the glacier afterwards saves a lot of time.
  2. DEM Assisted Coregistration (DEM-Assisted-Coregistration, Radar > Coregistration > DEM-Assisted Coregistration; help). The first product is the Reference. Defaults: DEM Copernicus 30m Global DEM, Resampling Type BISINC_5_POINT_INTERPOLATION, Mask out areas with no elevation on. Orbits and DEM alone align stable ground, so the remaining offsets are the surface motion. Offset Tracking stops if its input is not a coregistered stack.
  3. Offset Tracking (Offset-Tracking, Radar > SAR Applications > Ice; help). Parameters that matter:
    • Grid Azimuth/Range Spacing in Pixels: 40 × 40 by default. Smaller spacing gives more detail and longer run times.
    • Registration Window Width/Height: 128 × 128 by default. The window must contain recognisable texture and be clearly larger than the expected offset in pixels.
    • Cross-Correlation Threshold: 0.1 by default. Matches with a lower normalised correlation are rejected.
    • Max Velocity (m/day): 5.0 by default. Offsets larger than this velocity times the time interval are treated as outliers. Raise it for fast outlet glaciers; lower it to clean a slow landslide.
    • Spatial Average (box 5) and Fill Holes (radius 4) are on by default.
    The output band Velocity_<suffix> (unit m/day) is the magnitude of the range and azimuth offsets in metres divided by the time interval in days. For the direction, open the Velocity vector data node: each point carries heading, range_shift and azimuth_shift. To draw the offsets as arrows, open the velocity band and add the Coregistered GCP Movement Vector layer in the Layer Manager.
  4. Range-Doppler Terrain Correction (Terrain-Correction; help) of the velocity band, to put it on a map.

Check the result

  • Stable ground (rock outcrops, moraines) should show velocities near zero. Its spread is your noise level: roughly the matching error in pixels times the pixel spacing, divided by the number of days. With 10 m pixels, a 0.1-pixel error over 12 days is about 0.08 m/day.
  • Vectors should point downhill and follow the flow lines of the glacier.
  • Holes and smears: large filled areas mean that matching failed (no texture, surface change); the filled values are interpolated, not measured.

Common pitfalls

  • A registration window smaller than the expected offset. The true peak lies outside the search, and the velocity is wrong or rejected.
  • Leaving Max Velocity at 5 m/day on a glacier that moves faster. The fast centre is removed as outliers and filled from the slow margins.
  • Reading the velocity as three-dimensional. It is the magnitude of the 2-D offset in ground range and azimuth; vertical motion (and terrain not removed by the DEM) leaks into the range offset.
  • Pairs from different tracks or modes. Their geometries differ and the offsets contain parallax.
