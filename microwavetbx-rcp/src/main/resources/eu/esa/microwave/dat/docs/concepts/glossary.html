SAR Concepts: SAR Glossary

SAR Glossary

Short definitions of the SAR terms used across the Microwave Toolbox help. Each entry links to the concept page that explains it in full. Greek symbols are listed under their spelled-out names, for example σ0 under "Sigma nought".

A  B  C  D  E  F  G  H  I  L  M  N  O  P  Q  R  S  T  U  W  Y  Z

A

Abstracted Metadata. SNAP's sensor-independent metadata element. Each reader fills it from the product's own metadata (for example radar_frequency, PASS, antenna_pointing, range_spacing, srgr_flag), and the operators read their inputs from it. See Abstracted Metadata.

Alpha angle (α). The mean scattering-type angle of the Cloude-Pottier eigen-decomposition of T3: about 0° for surfaces, 45° for a dipole or a random cloud of dipoles, 90° for a dihedral. See Polarimetric Decompositions.

Amplitude. The magnitude of a complex pixel, A = √(i² + q²). Sentinel-1 GRD products are read as Amplitude_<pol> bands. See SAR Fundamentals.

Anisotropy (A). (λ2 − λ3) / (λ2 + λ3), from the eigenvalues of T3. It compares the second and third scattering mechanisms and adds information mainly at medium and high entropy. See Polarimetric Decompositions.

Ascending / descending pass. The satellite moves roughly north (ascending) or south (descending). A right-looking sensor such as Sentinel-1 looks east on ascending and west on descending passes, so slopes and motion are seen from opposite sides. Stored as PASS in the Abstracted Metadata. See SAR Geometry.

Azimuth. The along-track image direction. A target's azimuth position is the time of its closest approach to the sensor (zero-Doppler time). Stripmap azimuth resolution is about half the antenna length, independent of range and wavelength. See SAR Fundamentals.

B

Back-geocoding. Orbit- and DEM-based coregistration used for Sentinel-1 TOPS pairs. Each Reference pixel is mapped to the ground with a DEM and the Reference orbit, then into the Secondary geometry with the Secondary orbit; ESD then removes the remaining azimuth offset. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

Baseline. The vector between the two antenna positions of an interferometric pair. Its component along the line of sight (B∥) mainly adds the flat-Earth phase; the perpendicular baseline B⊥ sets the sensitivity to height. The temporal baseline is the time between the acquisitions. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Beta nought (β0). Radar brightness: the backscatter normalised by the reference area in slant-range geometry. It needs no knowledge of the terrain and is the input to Terrain Flattening (bands Beta0_<pol>). See Radiometry.

Bragg scattering. Resonant scattering from surface waves whose wavelength is λB = λ / (2 sin θ). It dominates the sea echo at incidence angles of about 20° to 60°, which is why ocean backscatter follows the local wind. See Ocean SAR.

Burst. A short block of TOPS or ScanSAR data acquired while the beam dwells on one sub-swath (less than a second for Sentinel-1 IW); after focusing one IW burst covers about 3 s (about 20 km) of azimuth. Consecutive bursts overlap slightly in azimuth. From IPF 3.40 each burst has a relative burst ID tied to its ground position. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

C

C2, C3, C4 (covariance matrices). Window- or look-averaged outer products of the lexicographic target vector. C3 (3×3) assumes reciprocity, C4 keeps SHV and SVH separate, and C2 (2×2) describes dual-pol and compact-pol data. Diagonal bands are real powers (C11), off-diagonal ones are split into _real and _imag parts. See Polarimetry Primer.

Calibration. The conversion of digital numbers into backscatter coefficients (β0, σ0, γ0) using the calibration look-up tables of the product. See Radiometry.

CFAR (constant false alarm rate). A detector that compares each pixel with the statistics of a background ring outside a guard area, with a threshold set by the probability of false alarm (PFA). SNAP's ship detector uses a two-parameter Gaussian CFAR. See Ocean SAR.

Change detection (log-ratio). Comparison of images of the same place at different dates. Because speckle is multiplicative, change is measured with the ratio of intensities, usually as the log-ratio D = ln(I1/I2). See SAR Change Detection.

Closure phase. The sum φ12 + φ23 − φ13 around a triplet of dates. It is zero for a single point scatterer, non-zero for multilooked distributed scatterers (soil moisture, vegetation), and a non-zero multiple of 2π in unwrapped phase marks an unwrapping error. See InSAR Time Series.

CMOD. The family of C-band VV geophysical model functions (CMOD4, CMOD5, CMOD5.N) that relate σ0 to the 10 m wind speed, the wind direction relative to the look direction and the incidence angle. See Ocean SAR.

Coherence (γ). The magnitude of the normalised complex cross-correlation of two coregistered complex images, from 0 (unrelated) to 1 (identical). It is estimated over a window and is biased upward for few looks. SNAP writes it as coh_<tag>. See Interferometric Coherence.

Coherency matrix (T3, T4). The averaged outer product of the Pauli target vector, <kP kPH>. It holds the same information as C3; its diagonal is the Pauli RGB. See Polarimetry Primer.

Compact polarimetry. A dual-channel mode that transmits one circular polarisation and receives H and V coherently, keeping part of the co-polar information that a VV+VH product loses. In SNAP the real data come from RCM. See Dual-pol and Compact Polarimetry.

Co-polarised / cross-polarised. HH and VV are co-polarised channels (same transmit and receive polarisation); HV and VH are cross-polarised and come mainly from volume scattering. See SAR Fundamentals.

Coregistration. Resampling one or more Secondary images onto the Reference grid to sub-pixel accuracy, as required for interferometry and stacks. Stripmap data use Create Stack, Cross-Correlation and Warp (or DEM-assisted coregistration, not for Sentinel-1 SLC); Sentinel-1 TOPS uses back-geocoding plus ESD. See InSAR Fundamentals and Stripmap DInSAR workflow.

Critical baseline. The perpendicular baseline at which the range spectra of the two images no longer overlap and the interferogram is pure noise: about 6 km for Sentinel-1 IW, about 1.1 km for ERS. See InSAR Fundamentals.

D

Deburst. Joining the bursts of a TOPS sub-swath into one continuous image. TOPSAR-Deburst switches between bursts at the middle of each overlap without blending. In InSAR it comes after the interferogram. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

Decibel (dB). xdB = 10 log10(x). Backscatter must be averaged, filtered and resampled in linear units; a mean taken in dB is biased low (by 2.5 dB for single-look data). See Radiometry.

Decorrelation. Loss of coherence between two images. Its sources are geometric (baseline), Doppler, volume, temporal and thermal; the total coherence is roughly their product. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Degree of polarisation (m). The polarised fraction of the received power, from 1 (fully polarised) to 0 (fully depolarised), computed from C2 or from the Stokes vector. See Dual-pol and Compact Polarimetry.

DEM (digital elevation model). A raster of terrain heights used for terrain correction, terrain flattening, back-geocoding and topographic phase. SNAP's default is the Copernicus 30m Global DEM. See DEMs and Height References.

Deramping. Multiplying a TOPS burst by the conjugate of its known azimuth phase ramp so that its spectrum sits at baseband. It is needed before any complex resampling; the ramp is put back afterwards (reramping). See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

Displacement (line-of-sight). The change of the sensor-to-ground distance converted from unwrapped phase, d = −(λ/4π) · φ. In SNAP a positive value means motion towards the satellite (Phase to Displacement, SBAS Inversion, Horizontal/Vertical Motion). It is always relative to a reference area. See From Phase to Displacement.

Distributed scatterer (DS). A pixel that holds many small scatterers of similar strength (fields, debris, sparse vegetation). Each single interferogram is noisy, but the coherence matrix of all pairs can be exploited with phase linking. See InSAR Time Series.

Doppler centroid. The centre frequency of a target's azimuth spectrum. In TOPS it changes linearly along each burst, which is why bursts must be deramped and coregistered to about 0.001 pixel in azimuth. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

DpRVI. Dual-pol radar vegetation index, 1 − mβ, from the degree of polarisation and the dominant eigenvalue fraction of C2: near 0 for bare soil, towards 1 for dense vegetation. See Dual-pol and Compact Polarimetry.

Dual-pol. Acquisition of one co-polar and one cross-polar channel (VV+VH or HH+HV), as in Sentinel-1. It gives a C2 matrix but not the HH-VV phase, so surface and double-bounce scattering cannot be measured apart. See Dual-pol and Compact Polarimetry.

E

EAP (elevation antenna pattern) phase. Sentinel-1 products processed with IPF before 2.43 compensated only the gain of the elevation antenna pattern, not its phase. EAP-Phase-Correction adds the missing phase so that old and new SLCs can be combined. See Orbits and Timing Corrections.

EGM96 / EGM2008. Earth gravitational models that define the geoid (complete to degree 360 and 2190). The Copernicus DEM uses EGM2008 heights; SNAP's "Apply Earth Gravitational Model" switch for external DEMs always adds the EGM96 geoid. See DEMs and Height References.

Ellipsoid (WGS84). The smooth mathematical reference surface of the Earth. Orbits and the range-Doppler geometry use ellipsoidal heights h. See DEMs and Height References.

Ellipsoid correction. Geocoding without a DEM: Ellipsoid Correction GG interpolates the product's tie-point grids, Ellipsoid Correction RD uses one average scene height. Relief displacement stays in the result. See SAR Geometry.

Entropy (H). The randomness of the scattering, from 0 (one mechanism) to 1 (random), computed from the normalised eigenvalues of T3. Together with α it spans the H-α plane used for unsupervised classification. See Polarimetric Decompositions.

ENL (equivalent number of looks). (mean of I)² / variance of I, measured in linear intensity over a homogeneous area. It describes how much averaging an image has received; a Sentinel-1 IW GRDH product has about 4.4. See Speckle and Multilooking.

ESD (Enhanced Spectral Diversity). Estimation of the residual azimuth offset of a TOPS pair from the double-difference phase in the burst overlaps. It provides the 0.001-pixel azimuth accuracy that orbit-and-DEM coregistration alone cannot reach. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

ETAD (Extended Timing Annotation Dataset). A Sentinel-1 auxiliary product with gridded range and azimuth timing corrections (troposphere, ionosphere, solid Earth tides, Doppler-induced range shift, bistatic shift, FM-rate mismatch). It improves absolute geolocation; it is used for example for offset tracking, wide-area InSAR and in the GSLC chain. See Orbits and Timing Corrections.

F

Faraday rotation. Rotation of the polarisation plane of the wave by the ionosphere in the Earth's magnetic field. It grows with the square of the wavelength, so it matters at L- and P-band. It breaks the equality SHV = SVH and is corrected before polarimetric matrices are formed. See Polarimetry Primer.

Flat-Earth phase. The interferometric phase that a smooth reference surface (the ellipsoid) would produce. It gives dense, regular fringes in range and is removed by the Interferogram operator using the orbits. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Foreshortening. Compression of slopes facing the sensor into fewer range cells, when the slope angle is smaller than the incidence angle. Such slopes look bright. See SAR Geometry.

Freeman-Durden decomposition. A model-based decomposition of C3 into surface, double-bounce and volume powers, assuming reflection symmetry. See Polarimetric Decompositions.

Fringe. One full 2π colour cycle of interferometric phase. For deformation it is λ/2 of line-of-sight motion (about 2.8 cm at C-band); for topography it is the height of ambiguity. See InSAR Fundamentals.

G

Gamma nought (γ0). Backscatter normalised by the area perpendicular to the line of sight. On flat ground γ0E = β0 tan θE; Calibration's Gamma0_<pol> band is this ellipsoid value. See Radiometry.

Gamma nought, terrain-flattened (γ0T). β0 normalised by the local illuminated area Aγ,T computed from a DEM (γ0T = β0 Aβ/Aγ,T; Small, 2011), which removes the brightness that slopes add. Terrain Flattening writes it as Gamma0_<pol>, the same name Calibration uses for the ellipsoid value. See Radiometry.

GCP (ground control point). In stripmap coregistration, a pair of matching positions in the Reference and Secondary images found by Cross-Correlation. Warp fits a polynomial to the GCP offsets and resamples the Secondary. See Stripmap DInSAR workflow.

Geocoding / terrain correction. Mapping each pixel from radar coordinates (azimuth time, slant range) to a map grid by solving the range-Doppler equations with a DEM. In SNAP: Range Doppler Terrain Correction. See SAR Geometry.

Geoid. The equipotential surface of Earth's gravity field that best fits mean sea level. Its height above the ellipsoid, the geoid undulation N, ranges from about −105 m to +85 m; h ≈ H + N. See DEMs and Height References.

GMF (geophysical model function). An empirical function that gives σ0 over the sea as a function of wind speed, wind direction relative to the look direction and incidence angle; inverted for wind retrieval. See Ocean SAR.

Goldstein filter. An adaptive, FFT-based filter that reduces interferometric phase noise, and so the number of residues, before unwrapping. See Phase Unwrapping.

GRD (Ground Range Detected). A Level-1 product with amplitude only (phase discarded), multilooked and resampled to ground range on an ellipsoid, but still in radar layout and not map-projected. See SAR Fundamentals.

GSLC (Geocoded SLC). A complex product with its phase preserved, resampled onto a map grid with a DEM by GSLC-Terrain-Correction. Interferograms can then be formed directly on the map grid. See SAR Fundamentals and GSLC workflow.

H

Height of ambiguity (h2π). The height difference that produces one topographic fringe, h2π = λ R sin θ / (2 B⊥) for repeat-pass InSAR. A small value means high height sensitivity. See InSAR Fundamentals.

HH, HV, VH, VV. Polarisation channels named transmit-then-receive, with H horizontal and V vertical. See SAR Fundamentals.

I

i / q bands. The in-phase (real) and quadrature (imaginary) parts of a complex pixel, stored in SNAP as i_ and q_ bands, for example i_IW1_VV. See SAR Fundamentals.

IEM (Integral Equation Model). A physical model of co-polarised backscatter from a randomly rough dielectric surface (Fung, Li and Chen, 1992), inverted by SNAP's soil moisture tools. See Soil Moisture from SAR.

Incidence angle (θ). The angle between the line of sight and the normal of the Earth ellipsoid. It grows across the swath, about 29° to 46° for Sentinel-1 IW. See SAR Geometry.

Intensity. The squared amplitude, I = i² + q². The virtual Intensity_ band of a raw product is not calibrated. See SAR Fundamentals.

Interferogram. The product of the Reference image with the complex conjugate of the Secondary. Its phase measures the difference in two-way path, φ = (4π/λ)(Rsec − Rref) modulo 2π. SNAP writes i_ifg, q_ifg and a virtual Phase_ifg band. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Ionospheric phase. The dispersive phase advance caused by the free electrons of the ionosphere, proportional to the change in total electron content (TEC) and to 1/f. It is largest at L- and P-band and is estimated with the split-spectrum method. See Atmospheric and Ionospheric Phase.

IPF (Instrument Processing Facility). The ground processor that generates Sentinel-1 Level-1 and Level-2 products. Its version is recorded in the product and decides which corrections apply, for example EAP phase before IPF 2.43, border-noise masking from IPF 2.90 and burst IDs from IPF 3.40. See Orbits and Timing Corrections.

IW, EW, SM, WV. Sentinel-1 acquisition modes: Interferometric Wide swath (TOPS, three sub-swaths, about 250 km), Extra Wide swath (TOPS, five sub-swaths), Stripmap, and Wave mode (small vignettes over the open ocean). See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

L

Layover. When a slope facing the sensor is steeper than the incidence angle, its top is closer to the sensor than its foot and is imaged in front of it, folded over the ground. Value 1 of Terrain Correction's layoverShadowMask. See SAR Geometry.

Line of sight (LOS). The direction from the ground to the satellite. InSAR measures only the projection of the ground motion on it: it is most sensitive to vertical and east-west motion and nearly blind to north-south motion. See From Phase to Displacement.

Local incidence angle. The angle between the line of sight and the normal of the local terrain, taken from a DEM. The projected local incidence angle keeps only the range slope. See SAR Geometry.

Look. One independent estimate of the backscatter of a resolution cell. Averaging L looks reduces the relative standard deviation of speckle by 1/√L. See Speckle and Multilooking.

M

Master / slave. Older names for Reference and Secondary. The help now uses Reference/Secondary, but some identifiers keep the old words, for example the operator alias MultiMasterInSAR (shown as Multi-Reference InSAR) and the legacy _mst / _slv band tags, which SNAP still accepts in place of _ref / _sec. See Reference / Secondary.

Multilooking. Averaging adjacent pixels in intensity. N independent pixels reduce the speckle variance by N and coarsen the resolution by the same factor; the range and azimuth looks also set the pixel shape. See Speckle and Multilooking.

N

NESZ (noise-equivalent sigma zero). The σ0 at which the signal power equals the thermal noise power. Sentinel-1 is specified at −22 dB or better. Dark targets and cross-polarised channels are often close to it. See Radiometry.

Network (interferogram network). The set of pairs formed from a stack: a single-reference ("star") network for persistent scatterers, or a small-baseline network for SBAS. It must be connected for the inversion to be well-posed. See InSAR Time Series.

O

OCN. The Sentinel-1 Level-2 ocean product. It has three components: ocean wind field (OWI), ocean swell spectra (OSW) and surface radial velocity (RVL). See Ocean SAR.

Offset tracking. Measuring the pixel offsets between two coregistered images by cross-correlation of image patches, to map large motion such as glacier flow, where the phase is decorrelated or too ambiguous. See Offset Tracking workflow.

Orbit state vectors. Satellite positions and velocities at regular times in an Earth-fixed frame. Sentinel-1 precise orbits (AUX_POEORB) are available after about 20 days, restituted orbits (AUX_RESORB) after about 3 hours. See Orbits and Timing Corrections.

Orthometric height (H). Height above the geoid, the "height above sea level" of maps and of most DEMs. See DEMs and Height References.

P

Pauli decomposition / Pauli RGB. Display of the diagonal of T3: red |SHH − SVV|²/2 (double bounce), green 2|SHV|² (volume), blue |SHH + SVV|²/2 (odd bounce). Bands Pauli_r, Pauli_g, Pauli_b. See Polarimetry Primer.

Perpendicular baseline (B⊥). The component of the baseline at right angles to the line of sight. It sets the height sensitivity of an interferogram, and the geometric decorrelation grows with it. See Baseline and InSAR Fundamentals.

Persistent scatterer (PS). A pixel dominated by one strong, stable reflector (a building corner, a rock face) whose phase stays coherent over long temporal and perpendicular baselines. See InSAR Time Series.

Phase. The angle of a complex pixel, φ = atan2(q, i). It holds the two-way path phase 4πR/λ plus a random scattering phase, so it is useful only when two images are compared. See SAR Fundamentals.

Phase linking. Estimation of one consistent phase per date from the coherence matrix of all pairs, computed over statistically homogeneous pixels (SNAP offers EVD and EMI, an approximation of the maximum-likelihood estimator). Used for distributed scatterers. See InSAR Time Series.

Phase unwrapping. Restoring the unknown whole cycles (2πk) of the wrapped phase so that it becomes a continuous measurement. SNAP uses SNAPHU. See Phase Unwrapping.

PRF (pulse repetition frequency). The rate at which the radar transmits pulses, which is the azimuth sampling rate of the raw data. It must be high enough to sample the azimuth Doppler bandwidth and low enough for the echoes of the whole swath to return between pulses. See SAR Fundamentals.

Q

Quad-pol (fully polarimetric). Acquisition of all four channels HH, HV, VH and VV with their relative phases, which gives the full scattering matrix S (for example RADARSAT-2, ALOS-2, BIOMASS). See Polarimetry Primer.

R

Range. The across-track image direction. Slant range R = cτ/2 follows from the echo delay τ; range resolution is c/(2B) for chirp bandwidth B. See SAR Fundamentals.

Reciprocity. For a monostatic radar the two cross-polar elements are equal, SHV = SVH, so the scattering matrix has three independent complex elements. See Polarimetry Primer.

Reference / Secondary. In a stack or pair, the Reference image defines the geometry that the Secondary images are coregistered to, and the interferogram is Reference × conj(Secondary). SNAP tags the bands _ref and _sec<n>, for example i_IW1_VV_ref_03Apr2025. Older pages and identifiers say master/slave. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Reflection symmetry. The property of many natural media that co-polar and cross-polar channels are uncorrelated, so four real elements of C3 vanish. Assumed by the Freeman-Durden and van Zyl decompositions. See Polarimetry Primer.

Residue. A 2×2 pixel loop whose wrapped phase differences sum to ±1 cycle. Paths that pass on either side of it disagree by 2π, so the unwrapper must place discontinuities between residues. See Phase Unwrapping.

Roll invariance. A quantity is roll-invariant if it does not change when the target is rotated about the line of sight, for example the span, entropy, anisotropy and α. See Polarimetry Primer.

S

SBAS (small baseline subset). A time-series method that forms only pairs with short temporal and perpendicular baselines, unwraps them, and inverts the network pixel by pixel for the phase at each date. See InSAR Time Series.

ScanSAR. A wide-swath mode in which the beam switches between several sub-swaths in bursts, at coarser azimuth resolution. TOPS is a ScanSAR variant. See SAR Fundamentals.

Shadow. A back slope steeper than 90° − θ is hidden from the beam and contains only noise. Value 2 of Terrain Correction's layoverShadowMask (3 = layover and shadow). See SAR Geometry.

SHP (statistically homogeneous pixels). Neighbours of a pixel whose amplitude time series pass a two-sample test (KS, AD or TLog in SNAP); phase linking averages over them. See InSAR Time Series.

Sigma nought (σ0). Backscatter normalised by the reference area on the ground. On flat ground σ0E = β0 sin θE. It is Calibration's default output, Sigma0_<pol>. See Radiometry.

Sinclair matrix (S). The 2×2 complex scattering matrix [SHH SHV; SVH SVV] measured by a quad-pol radar for one resolution cell. See Polarimetry Primer.

Slant range / ground range. The radar samples equal steps in slant range (along the line of sight); on flat ground a slant step Δr covers a ground step Δr / sin θ. SLC products are in slant range, GRD products in ground range. See SAR Geometry.

SLC (Single Look Complex). A Level-1 product with complex i/q pixels at full resolution, in slant range and zero-Doppler azimuth. Needed for interferometry and polarimetry. See SAR Fundamentals.

SNAPHU. A statistical-cost, network-flow phase unwrapper (Chen and Zebker). SNAP exports the wrapped phase and coherence for it and imports the result as Unw_Phase_ifg_<ref-date>_<sec-date> (for a stack with more than one Secondary the band keeps the name UnwPhase_ifg_<tag>); the default cost mode is DEFO. See Phase Unwrapping.

Span. The total power of the scattering matrix, |SHH|² + |SHV|² + |SVH|² + |SVV|², equal to the trace of C3 or T3. See Polarimetry Primer.

Speckle. The grainy texture of SAR images, caused by the interference of echoes from many scatterers in one resolution cell. It is multiplicative, and single-look intensity is exponentially distributed. See Speckle and Multilooking.

Split-spectrum method. Estimation of the ionospheric phase from interferograms formed in a low and a high range sub-band, exploiting its 1/f dependence. See Atmospheric and Ionospheric Phase.

Stokes vector. Four real parameters g0 to g3 that describe the polarisation state of the received wave in compact polarimetry. See Dual-pol and Compact Polarimetry.

Stripmap. The basic SAR mode: a fixed beam images one continuous strip. See SAR Fundamentals.

Sub-swath. One of the beam positions in elevation of a TOPS or ScanSAR acquisition: IW1 to IW3 in Sentinel-1 IW, EW1 to EW5 in EW. Each sub-swath is stored as a separate image, and band names include it (for example i_IW1_VV). See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

Swath. The strip of ground imaged by the radar, from near range to far range. See SAR Fundamentals.

T

T3, T4. See Coherency matrix.

Temporal coherence (γT). A measure, from 0 to 1, of how well the phases estimated by phase linking or SBAS fit the observed pairwise phases. See InSAR Time Series.

Terrain flattening. Radiometric correction that normalises β0 by the DEM-derived illuminated area, giving γ0T. See Radiometry.

Thermal noise removal. Subtraction of the annotated noise power from the intensity. It improves dark areas and cross-pol channels but destroys the phase, so it is not used before InSAR or polarimetry. See Radiometry.

TOPS (Terrain Observation by Progressive Scans). A ScanSAR mode in which the beam also sweeps from backward to forward in azimuth during each burst, used by Sentinel-1 IW and EW. The sweep gives each burst a Doppler ramp. See Sentinel-1 TOPS.

Topographic phase. The part of the interferometric phase caused by terrain height above the reference surface, proportional to the perpendicular baseline. It is removed with a DEM. See InSAR Fundamentals.

Tropospheric delay. The non-dispersive delay of the signal in the neutral atmosphere: a stratified part that correlates with topography and a turbulent part from water vapour. Changes between dates appear as phase that looks like motion. See Atmospheric and Ionospheric Phase.

U

Unwrapped phase. Phase with the whole cycles restored, φu = φw + 2πk. It is known only up to an additive constant, so values are relative to a reference area. In SNAP, band names start with Unw (Unw_Phase_ifg_ from SNAPHU Import, UnwPhase_ifg_ from Batch Snaphu Unwrap and from SNAPHU Import on a stack with more than one Secondary). SNAPHU Import and Batch Snaphu Unwrap both set the unit abs_phase. See Phase Unwrapping.

W

Water Cloud Model. A vegetation model that treats a crop canopy as a cloud of water droplets that adds its own backscatter and attenuates the soil contribution. See Soil Moisture from SAR.

Wishart distribution. The distribution of a look-averaged polarimetric covariance or coherency matrix of a uniform area. It underlies the Wishart classifiers and Polarimetric Change Detection. See Wishart Statistics and Classification.

Wrapped phase. The observed interferometric phase, known only modulo 2π and shown in (−π, π]. See Phase Unwrapping.

Y

Yamaguchi decomposition. A model-based decomposition that adds a helix component to the Freeman-Durden surface, double-bounce and volume terms, for areas without reflection symmetry such as cities. See Polarimetric Decompositions.

Z

Zero-Doppler. The focusing convention that places a target at the azimuth time of its closest approach, when the line of sight is perpendicular to the platform velocity. See SAR Geometry.
