Import CIMR Level-1B

CIMR (Copernicus Imaging Microwave Radiometer) is a passive microwave radiometer, not a radar. The CIMR-L1B reader opens CIMR Level-1B products for viewing in SNAP. The SAR operators of the Microwave Toolbox do not apply to these data.

Mission

  • Programme: Copernicus Expansion mission, developed by ESA for the European Union.
  • Instrument: conically scanning radiometer with an 8.5 m reflector, viewing at a zenith angle of 52 to 55°. Dual-polarisation channels at 1.4 GHz (L), 6.9 GHz (C), 10.65 GHz (X), 18.7 GHz (K; KU_BAND in the product) and 36.5 GHz (Ka), with footprints of about 58, 15, 15, 5 and 4.5 km.
  • Orbit: dawn-dusk sun-synchronous, 817 km altitude, 29-day repeat cycle; swath about 1900 km.
  • Status: not yet launched.

How to open

Use File > Open Product and select the .nc file. The reader recognises the file only by its name, which must follow the CIMR L1B naming convention: it starts with W_, contains -SAT-CIMR-1B_C_ and ends with .nc. A renamed file is not recognised.

What you get

  • The swath samples are placed, by nearest neighbour, on a regular latitude/longitude grid with 0.02° cells that covers the product. The product therefore has a map (CRS) geocoding.
  • Bands are named <BAND>_<variable>_feed_<n>, for example C_BAND_brightness_temperature_h_feed_1. L-band has one feed, C- and X-band four, Ku- and Ka-band eight. Bands are grouped by frequency band and feed.
  • Variables include the brightness temperatures in H and V polarisation and the third and fourth Stokes parameters (t3, t4), in kelvin, both with RFI mitigation (brightness_temperature_*) and without (raw_brightness_temperature_*). Further bands hold the uncertainty (tsu), the radiometric resolution (nedt), the Faraday rotation angle, the total electron content, the water fraction, the footprint size and orientation, and instrument status flags.
  • For each band and feed, a <BAND>_coverage_feed_<n> band and mask show which grid cells received a sample. Floating-point bands use NaN as no-data.
  • The NetCDF global and group attributes are in the metadata tree.

Known limitations

  • The 0.02° grid is much finer than the instrument footprints, so neighbouring cells repeat the same sample. Coverage gaps between scans are marked by the coverage masks.
  • The band definitions are tied to the product's format_version attribute. The reader knows version 1.1 and files without the attribute; files of another version do not open.
