Import NISAR

NISAR is a joint NASA–ISRO mission carrying an L-band and an S-band SAR. SNAP reads NISAR HDF5 products with the NISAR reader.

Mission

  • Operators: NASA/JPL (L-band SAR) and ISRO (S-band SAR).
  • Radar: L-band (wavelength about 24 cm) and S-band (about 9.4 cm). A product file holds one band of the two (LSAR or SSAR).
  • Orbit: 747 km, 12-day exact repeat cycle.
  • Lifetime: launched 30 July 2025.

Which product to choose: GCOV for backscatter and land-cover work; RSLC for your own interferometry or polarimetry in SNAP; GSLC for interferometry on a common map grid; RUNW or GUNW when the NISAR interferogram is enough.

How to open

Select the .h5 file. Its name must start with NISAR. If you select an .xml file with the same base name, the reader opens the .h5 next to it.

What you get

  • RSLC and GSLC: i_/q_ bands per polarisation (for example i_HH) with virtual Intensity_ and Phase_ bands. (Earlier versions of the reader named the GSLC phase band PhaseHH; it is now Phase_HH, as for RSLC, so graphs that refer to the old name need updating.)
  • GCOV: the covariance terms, auxiliary layers and, where the terms allow, a C3 or C2 matrix. See GCOV bands.
  • Interferogram and offset products: layers such as unwrappedPhase, coherenceMagnitude and connectedComponents, with the polarisation appended (for example coherenceMagnitude_HH). The complex wrapped interferogram is read as i_/q_ bands with its phase in a virtual band (GUNW wrappedInterferogram_HH, with its coherence wrappedCoherenceMagnitude_HH; RIFG Phase_HH). RIFG and RUNW also give the digitalElevationModel used by the processor, and its coarser copy on the pixel-offset grid, digitalElevationModel_pixelOffsets.
  • Many acquisitions carry a main band (frequency A) and a narrow second band (frequency B). Bands of frequency B get the suffix _S, for example i_HH_S. Frequency B has a coarser pixel spacing (for example 80 m against 20 m in GCOV, 25 m against 6 m slant range in RSLC), so such a product holds rasters of two sizes, each with its own geocoding. Other layers on their own grid are geocoded the same way: the 20 m wrapped interferogram of a GUNW, whose other layers are at 80 m, and the coarser pixel offsets of RIFG and RUNW. Each such grid also has its own incidence angle, incident_angle with the grid's suffix (incident_angle_S for frequency B), which Calibration uses for its bands.
  • Level-2 products are geocoded from their coordinate arrays, in latitude/longitude or UTM. Level-1 products are geocoded from the metadata geolocation grid at the ellipsoid (0 m height); use Terrain Correction for map-accurate positions.

GCOV bands

A GCOV term XYZW is the multilooked product ⟨SXY SZW*⟩, already radiometrically terrain corrected (γ0) and geocoded.

BandContent
HHHH, HVHV, VHVH, VVVV, RHRH, … Backscatter power (γ0, linear) of one channel
HHHV_real, HHHV_imag, … Real and imaginary parts of a cross term. The product lists the terms it carries: 10 for quad-pol, 2 or 3 for dual-pol.
mask0: a sample in the averaging window was partially focused or invalid; 1…n: the subswath of a valid sample; 255: outside the swath
numberOfLooksEquivalent number of looks of each pixel
rtcGamma0ToSigma0FactorMultiply a power term by this factor to get σ0
inputDataExceptionMaskProcessor bit flags on the input data

Pixels outside the swath are NaN, the bands' no-data value. The gcov_valid mask (gcov_valid_S for frequency B) selects the samples the product marks valid, mask 1…n. It is not applied to the bands: samples with mask 0 still hold data, about a fifth of the footprint in current products. To exclude them, use gcov_valid as a band's valid-pixel expression or in Band Maths.

Polarimetric matrix

The reader adds virtual matrix bands so the polarimetric operators accept GCOV directly:

  • Quad-pol: C3 bands C11, C12_real, …, C33 on k = [SHH, √2 SX, SVV]. NISAR keeps HV and VH separate; the reader averages them, SX = (SHV + SVH)/2:
    • C11 = HHHH, C13 = HHVV, C33 = VVVV
    • C12 = (HHHV + HHVH)/√2
    • C22 = (HVHV + VHVH + 2 Re HVVH)/2
    • C23 = (HVVV + VHVV)/√2
    A product with only one of HV and VH uses it for SX.
  • Dual-pol with a cross term: C2 bands, for example C11 = HHHH, C12 = HHHV, C22 = HVHV. Dual-pol products with only the power terms (HHHH, HVHV) get no matrix.
  • Compact-pol (RHRH, RVRV, RHRV): C2 bands, with the abstracted-metadata compact_mode set from the transmit polarisation (R or L circular).

Products with a matrix are flagged polarimetric in the abstracted metadata (polsar_data = 1, sample type COMPLEX). The matrix is built for frequency A only, so the polarimetric operators do not process frequency B. Its _S power terms can still be kept with Band Select and used as backscatter. The abstracted metadata still describes the matrix product (polarimetric, sample type COMPLEX), so operators that look for a matrix reject a product with only those bands.

Typical next steps; GCOV needs no calibration, multilooking or terrain correction:

  • Polarimetric Speckle Filter (optional, GCOV is already multilooked), then Polarimetric Decomposition (H-A-α, Freeman-Durden, …; H-α dual pol for C2) or Polarimetric Matrices for T3.
  • Compact-pol: Compact Pol Stokes Parameters or Compact Pol Decomposition.
  • Polarimetric Classification: Wishart on C3, or the dual-pol Cloude-Pottier and H-α Wishart on C2.

The matrix bands are virtual. BEAM-DIMAP stores them as expressions; other formats such as GeoTIFF write their values, making the file about a third to a half larger. GeoTIFF also needs bands of one size: keep one frequency first.

The mission and acquisition parameters SNAP uses are listed in the Abstracted Metadata.

Known limitations

  • Only RSLC can be radiometrically calibrated. Calibration rejects the other products: GCOV is already calibrated, GSLC is calibrated by the NISAR processor, and the interferometric, offset and soil-moisture products are not backscatter.
  • Operators that need all bands the same size reject a product with both frequencies. Keep one frequency first with Band Select or Subset. The abstracted metadata (radar frequency, pixel spacing, bandwidth) describes frequency A.
  • An RSLC with both frequencies can be subset and saved as BEAM-DIMAP. Subset it again after re-opening the saved file only after keeping one frequency: BEAM-DIMAP does not keep a tie-point grid's own geocoding, so a further subset of both frequencies misplaces frequency B.
  • When subsetting a product with both frequencies, keep the frequency B tie-point grids (latitude_S, longitude_S, incident_angle_S) selected. SNAP versions without the fix for this case stop with a NullPointerException if they are left out.
  • RIFG, RUNW and ROFF over the poles (polar stereographic grids) open without geocoding.
  • Current SME2 soil-moisture products (2026 layout) cannot be read yet.
  • The reader also accepts files named WINNIP*, the pre-launch simulated products in the same layout.

Recommended next step

  • RSLC: Calibration, then Multilook and Range-Doppler Terrain Correction, or Create Stack and Interferogram Formation for InSAR.
  • GCOV with a matrix: Polarimetric Decomposition, after the optional Polarimetric Speckle Filter. GCOV without one (single-pol or power-only dual-pol): Speckle Filter. No terrain correction is needed. See GCOV bands.
